# Structured Methods

Use this reference only when the selected route includes a method not covered by an upstream method card.

## TRIZ

1. State the contradiction as: improving **A** makes **B** worse.
2. Identify resources already present in the system, including time, space, information, energy, and unused outputs.
3. Seek separation in time, space, condition, or scale before accepting a compromise.
4. Generate concepts with a small relevant subset of inventive principles, such as segmentation, extraction, local quality, asymmetry, merging, universality, nesting, prior action, dynamics, feedback, intermediary, or parameter change.
5. Reject concepts that merely move the cost or failure to another stakeholder without acknowledging it.

Do not reproduce the full 39-parameter contradiction matrix from memory. If an exact matrix lookup matters, use an authoritative licensed reference or clearly label the chosen principles as heuristic.

## SIT and ASIT

Work inside the closed world of existing components and nearby resources.

- Subtraction: remove an apparently essential component and redesign around its absence.
- Division: split a component or process, then rearrange it across time, space, or responsibility.
- Task unification: make an existing resource perform an additional job.
- Multiplication: copy a component but change an important attribute of the copy.
- Attribute dependency: make one property vary with another condition.

For ASIT, describe the desired end state first, then use only resources already inside or immediately adjacent to the problem.

## Morphological Analysis

1. Define three to seven independent parameters.
2. List two to five meaningfully different options per parameter.
3. Build combinations across parameters; do not enumerate the full Cartesian product when it is huge.
4. Eliminate physically, legally, or operationally impossible combinations with explicit reasons.
5. Select diverse combinations rather than several near-duplicates.

## First Principles

Separate verified constraints from conventions and inherited assumptions. Reconstruct the solution from the minimum necessary functions, resources, and laws. Treat policy or organizational constraints as real until the user authorizes changing them.

## Reverse Thinking

Ask how to guarantee the opposite outcome, list the mechanisms that would cause it, then invert or neutralize those mechanisms. Do not turn this into unsafe instructions; keep harmful examples abstract and prevention-focused.

## Rapid Ideation

Time-box generation and defer judgment. For solo work, use a Crazy 8s-style set of eight distinct concepts. For groups, prefer silent brainwriting before discussion so participation is not dominated by louder voices.

## Random and Forced Connections

Choose a neutral external object, service, or operating model. Extract two or three mechanisms from it, then map each mechanism to the target problem. Keep only connections with a plausible causal path.
